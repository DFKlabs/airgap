Here's how you turn data into sound… and how the phone knows nothing got lost.
It's called frequency-shift keying. Each pitch stands for a number. Use sixteen pitches, and every single tone carries four bits.
Each tone plays for seventy milliseconds. Then silence, so echoes from the room fade before the next one.
But how does the receiver know a message is starting? A preamble. A fixed pattern it's always listening for.
And the genius part: a checksum. A small number calculated from the whole message. If one tone is misheard, the numbers don't match… and the message is rejected.
Same idea as old dial-up modems. Just in your pocket.
